# Planning Method

## Purpose and boundaries

Turn “I want a semiconductor job” into a set of demonstrable capabilities. Choose roles through actual tasks, resources, and interests, rather than counting lectures watched or resources collected. The times and levels below are educational design recommendations, not hiring statistics or offer promises.

## Minimum student profile

Record enrollment month, program length, graduation month, major/research direction, supervisor's topic, graduation requirements, net weekly study hours, main language, current and target locations, work preferences, coursework, programming background, project evidence, available software/instruments, internship policy, and the nearest critical deadlines.

Allow “unknown” or “prefer not to disclose”. Use cities to filter job locations, not infer family circumstances. Distinguish software that is authorized and available, software known only conceptually, and unavailable software. Installation is not evidence of proficiency. Date each profile entry.

## Diagnostic method

Assess four dimensions: principles, execution, debugging, and explanation. Do not replace a capability profile with one total score. Students may use documentation, but should record the assistance received. Observe whether they can explain key parameters, predict changes, identify anomalies, and reproduce experiments.

| Level | Observable behavior | What this does not justify claiming |
| --- | --- | --- |
| L0 | Confirmed no exposure, or a diagnostic shows the introductory task cannot yet be completed | Missing uploaded evidence alone means “not yet assessed” |
| L1 | Explains principles and completes basic calculations/reading exercises | Independent engineering implementation |
| L2 | Completes an experiment with documentation and explains inputs/outputs | Independent design or signoff |
| L3 | Independently delivers a module, diagnoses failures, and provides reproducible material | Automatic claims of production/tape-out experience |
| L4 | Compares and optimizes under several constraints, with review | Seniority or professional certification |

Before diagnosis, use “not yet assessed”, not an automatic L0. Adjust suggested target levels to the role and available time. A useful planning target is L2–L3 in core P0 skills with at least one L3 artifact; the target JD still determines employer standards.

Diagnostic examples:

- Analog: explain why operating point affects gain; sketch how a first-order pole changes; infer bias or swing issues from distorted output.
- Digital: write a register with reset/enable; explain setup/hold; find a boundary a testbench does not cover.
- Devices/process: explain threshold/subthreshold behavior; check plot units and axes; identify confounded controls or process variables.
- Test/reliability: distinguish accuracy from precision; interpret data under stated test conditions; explain limits of extrapolating a small sample.

For each question record the question, response, resources used, basis of assessment, and remedial task. Do not generalize one answer into “this person is unsuitable for this career”.

## Role choice and reversible exploration

Retain 2–3 candidates in the first month. Spend 4–8 hours on a microtask for each, comparing enjoyment, independent output, difficulties, and resources. Parallel small tasks belong only in exploration; the formal route has one primary track and one alternative.

Suggested discussion weights: interest and tolerance 25%, existing foundation 20%, research reuse 20%, resource access 20%, and location/work-pattern fit 15%. These are adjustable weights. Use low/medium/high or 1–5 ratings with evidence. Mark missing dimensions unknown; do not force a total or infer offer probability.

Reasons to change track include still disliking core tasks after at least two practical attempts, prolonged lack of critical resources, an irreconcilable graduation-research conflict, or a gap between real JD requirements and available time that is too large. Prefer adjacent roles and list retained versus additional skills.

## Work backward from graduation

The three-year example starts at enrollment month M1, not a fixed calendar year:

| Stage | Main outputs | Condition for moving forward |
| --- | --- | --- |
| M1–M2 | Profile, candidate roles, diagnosis, minimal tool check | Explain role-choice rationale and resource boundaries |
| M3–M6 | Primary track, P0 foundations, one minimal project | Explain principles and independently repeat key experiments |
| M7–M12 | Core project V1, research-to-skill mapping, first mock interview | Reproducible specifications/results and failure records |
| M13–M18 | Project V2, adjacent alternative, internship preparation | At least one L3 evidence item; resume claims withstand follow-ups |
| M19–M24 | Approved internship or university-based alternative, portfolio | Real tasks or a reviewable alternative project completed |
| M25–M30 | Recruitment for the relevant graduating cohort, interview review, graduation progress | Every tailored resume maps to role evidence |
| M31–M36 | Graduation/onboarding transition and delivery skills | School graduation requirements and agreed employment arrangements met |

If recruiting begins earlier, move preparation forward; verify autumn/spring recruitment dates employer by employer. For two years: M1 positioning; M2–M6 foundations plus core project; M7–M12 project/internship preparation alongside actual recruitment; M13–M18 applications/interviews and the main graduation work; M19–M24 graduation and handover. For two and a half years, interpolate against graduation month, preserving graduation work and the core project rather than mechanically compressing everything.

## Time budget

H is sustainable net time after required classes, group meetings, mandatory research hours, and everyday commitments. Allocate at most 0.8H and reserve 0.2H; reduce further if the user prefers lower intensity.

| Net time H | Concepts/exercises | Practical project | Review/job materials | Buffer | Strategy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 6 hours | 1.5 | 2.5 | 0.8 | 1.2 | One microproject; extend milestones |
| 10 hours | 2.5 | 4.5 | 1 | 2 | Primary foundations and one project |
| 15 hours | 3.5 | 7 | 1.5 | 3 | Iterate a core project continuously |
| 20 hours | 4.5 | 9 | 2.5 | 4 | Add reassessment, not another primary track |

These rows are complete budgets; add no hidden assignments. Examination, paper-deadline, or lab-roster weeks may become maintenance weeks containing only review and evidence preservation. Do not recommend sustained sleep sacrifice to maintain progress.

If prerequisites are missing, reduce task size: concept → single-variable experiment → connecting two modules → system project. Schedule no more than 3 main concurrent tasks per week. Ideally each takes 1–3 hours; divide larger tasks into explicit checkpoints.

## Generic 90-day loop

Weeks 1–2: diagnosis and a minimal tool run. Weeks 3–4: address the two largest P0 gaps. Weeks 5–8: build a minimal version of the core project. Weeks 9–10: add boundary conditions and debugging. Week 11: organize evidence and explanations. Week 12: unaided reproduction, mock interview, and next-cycle revision. The remaining 6 days within 90 days are for retesting, organization, and buffer. Rewrite concrete tasks for the selected track; do not paste this schedule unchanged.

## Integrate research with employment preparation

Create three columns: school-mandated work → job capability needed → combined output. Device research may demonstrate automated parameter extraction and statistics, but not directly prove RTL design ability. Analog research simulations may demonstrate bias and stability analysis; without layout reports they cannot prove physical verification. Have the student discuss time, publication boundaries, and graduation progress with the supervisor monthly. The student conducts that communication.
