# Review and Recruiting

## Weekly review inputs

Ask the student for net time this week, original versus actual hours, output paths or key contents, acceptance results, blockers, and next week's classes/research commitments. A short text response is enough. Do not require disclosure of sensitive school or employer files.

Before assigning reassessment, identify whether existing results are DC, AC, small-signal noise, transient, digital timing, or physical measurements. For example, comparing input/output amplitudes in the same steady-state window is appropriate for certain transient experiments, not automatically for material containing only an AC frequency response. If the analysis type is unknown, inspect plot axes, excitation, and configuration first.

## Weekly review outputs

1. Verified progress, linked to skills and concrete evidence.
2. Unpassed items: what is missing, and whether the gap is prerequisite knowledge or execution.
3. Tasks retained, reduced, or delayed, with reasons and recomputed total hours.
4. At most 3 main tasks next week, plus buffer.
5. Next reassessment using different parameters or a new boundary so memorizing the answer is insufficient.

If less than roughly half the scheduled work is completed for two consecutive weeks, first check whether net availability was overestimated. Then remove advanced work while retaining graduation tasks and core P0. This fraction is a scheduling signal, not a punishment threshold. Adjust immediately for lab queues, supervisor assignments, or health changes; accumulating failures is unnecessary.

## Work backward from the recruiting window

Relative to a target recruiting window confirmed by the user: 6 months before, collect JD samples and address the core project; 3 months before, finish resume V1, project explanations, and technical reassessment; 1 month before, organize publishable work and practice interviews/application tracking; after opening, tailor the resume by role and review interviews. Coordinate internships with school policy, the supervisor's arrangements, and graduation milestones.

Without a known window, mark it “to be checked” and point the student to official employer graduate/internship pages and the school careers site. Do not claim an internship always occurs in a particular month of the second year. Do not automatically apply, contact HR, or make commitments for the student.

## JD matrix

For each JD record company/title, original URL, retrieval date, recruiting stage, location, mandatory conditions, duties, skills, tools, advantages, and source accessibility. Compare only after filtering conditions are explicit. Identical titles do not ensure identical duties.

Use skills as rows and JD1…JD5 as columns, with “explicit requirement”, “advantage”, or “not mentioned”. “Not mentioned” does not mean unnecessary. Use common requirements to inform P0 and personal targets to inform P1; do not treat sample frequency as industry statistics.

## Resume evidence chain

Suggested sentence: Under [conditions/scope], addressed [problem] by personally [action], validated through [method] to obtain [actual result], while identifying [limits]. Every number must be traceable; do not invent percentages for appearance.

Example of structure only; any numbers require actual results: “Built DC/AC/transient amplifier tests using authorized models, analyzed effects of bias and loading on gain/stability, and organized reproducible experiments and failure diagnoses.” Without actual work, the student cannot copy this as personal experience.

State personal responsibility clearly. For an open-source reproduction, write “implemented/verified this change based on project X”; do not claim ownership of the whole public repository. Leave absent tape-out, internship, publication, or award experience blank.

## Interview practice

Rotate role-relevant foundations/derivations, engineering tradeoffs, project details, debugging, tool/result limitations, and collaborative explanation. Use 3–5 questions per session. Follow-up depth matters more than question-bank size.

Example sequence:

1. Explain the problem and why its constraint exists in 90 seconds.
2. Draw the architecture or experiment flow and identify your contribution.
3. Describe an initial-version failure, how you diagnosed it, and the basis for a change.
4. Predict then explain the effect of changing a boundary condition.
5. Which results remain unverified? What is missing for an employer environment?

Feedback covers correctness, evidence, explanation, and boundaries. Replace generic praise with specific gaps. Reference answers are appropriate for practice, but let the student answer first in an assessment. If no real project failure occurred, retain that fact and use a separately labeled educational fault-injection case for question 3 rather than inventing a defect.

## Application ledger and alternatives

Record role, date, source, resume version, status, interview topics, and next action without collecting unnecessary sensitive details. Distinguish intended application, submitted, interviewing, and closed. AI may organize the ledger; actual applications/messages require explicit user authorization.

Examples of adjacent moves: RTL → DV retains HDL/protocol/debugging and adds verification plans/assertions/coverage; analog design → layout retains devices/circuits and adds placement, matching, parasitics, and actual physical verification; devices → process retains physics/data and adds experimental design, SPC, and process-flow understanding. An adjacent role still requires additional learning.
